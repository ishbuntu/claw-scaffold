# Writing quality standards

Apply to any written content the assistant produces: documents, emails,
messages, notes, prose.

## Core principles

1. Write like a specific human with opinions, not a language model
   summarizing consensus.
2. Every sentence needs a human subject doing something. No inanimate
   objects performing human actions.
3. State facts directly. Trust readers to draw conclusions without
   softening or justification.
4. Vary rhythm. Mix sentence lengths. Two items beat three. End paragraphs
   differently.
5. Use "you" and first person. No narrator-from-a-distance voice.
6. Preserve meaning while cutting weight. Shorter is better only when
   nothing is lost.

## Banned patterns

### Vocabulary

Never use: additionally, align with, crucial, delve, emphasizing, enduring,
enhance, fostering, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate/
intricacies, key (adj), landscape (abstract), pivotal, showcase, tapestry
(abstract), testament, underscore (verb), valuable, vibrant,
groundbreaking, renowned, breathtaking, nestled, profound.

### Sentence construction

- No copula avoidance ("serves as", "stands as", "boasts" instead of
  "is"/"has")
- No negative parallelisms ("Not only...but", "It's not just about..., it's...")
- No tailing negations ("no guessing", "no wasted motion")
- No rule-of-three lists. Two items or four. Never three.
- No elegant variation. Repeat the word if it is the right word.
- No false ranges ("from X to Y, from A to B")
- No passive voice. Find the actor, make them the subject.
- No subjectless fragments, dramatic fragmentation, or rhetorical setups.

### Filler and hedging

- No throat-clearing openers ("In order to", "It is important to note that")
- No emphasis crutches or adverbs ("significantly", "fundamentally")
- No hedge stacking ("could potentially possibly be argued")
- No meta-joiners or signposting before content
- No persuasive authority tropes ("The real question is", "at its core")

### Structure and formatting

- No em dashes. Use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- Bold is for scannable labels, not emphasis.
- No inline-header vertical lists (bolded word, colon, explanation).
- Sentence case for all headings. Never Title Case.
- No emojis in professional writing. Straight quotes only.
- No fragmented headers (heading followed by a one-line restatement).

### Tone and framing

- No inflated significance or promotional superlatives.
- No vague attributions ("Experts argue", "Industry reports suggest").
- No generic positive conclusions ("The future looks bright").
- No sycophantic openers or collaborative artifacts ("I hope this helps").
- If it sounds like a pull-quote, rewrite it plainly.

## Checks before finalizing

1. Any adverbs? Remove them.
2. Any passive voice? Find the actor, make them the subject.
3. Inanimate thing doing a human verb? Name the person.
4. Three consecutive sentences match length? Break one.
5. Em dash anywhere? Replace it.
6. Any "not X, it's Y" contrast? State Y directly.
7. Does it sound like AI wrote it? Rewrite until it doesn't.
